First, two big words

What is a District Assembly?

Imagine your neighbourhood as a huge team — the District Assembly is like the coaches and leaders of that team!

They are the local government people whose job is to take care of the town: fix roads, build schools, keep streets clean, and make sure everyone stays safe and happy.

Big word

Local government — The leaders in charge of your own district, rather than the whole country.

3

And the second one

What is a District Plan?

A district plan is like a giant map made by local leaders to make a town or city a much better place to live!

Instead of adults just guessing what to do, the plan lists the biggest problems — dirty streets, schools that need help, places that need safer parks — then writes a step-by-step checklist for fixing them.

The Effia-Kwesimintsim Municipal Assembly in the Western Region made its very own district plan — for every child, no matter who they are or where they live!

Big words

Effia-Kwesimintsim Municipal Assembly — The local government team in charge of Effia-Kwesimintsim, with its offices in Kwesimintsim, near Takoradi. People often call it EKMA.

Western Region — Ghana is split into sixteen big areas called regions. The Western Region is in the south-west, along the Atlantic coast.

Why did they make the plan?

So every child grows up happy, safe and ready to learn

Every child in Effia-Kwesimintsim deserves it — no matter who they are or where they live!

Our big dream: a community where EVERY child…

Plays, laughs and learns

In a safe and friendly space.

Can easily join in all the fun

Including children with disabilities.

Is loved, protected and kept safe

From all harm, at home and in the community.

Big word

Disabilities — When a body or brain works in its own way — like needing a wheelchair to get around, or extra help to see, hear or learn.

Understanding the plan

Our 3 Super Powers

To help every child grow up happy and safe, Effia-Kwesimintsim's leaders are focusing on three priority areas — and every one of them points back at the same thing.

Turn the page to meet all three!

Big word

Priority areas — The things that matter most, so they get done first.

Happy Playtime & Supportive Families

Psychosocial well-being

Lots of play, lots of friends — and happy, helpful homes!

Monthly play dates

Fun, creative play sessions every month, where children can make friends, play games and express themselves!

Parent clubs

Monthly parent meetings and friendly classes, so mums and dads can share parenting ideas and learn great ways to care for their little ones.

Big words

Psychosocial well-being — How you feel inside, and how well you get along with the people around you.

Express yourself — Show what you think and feel — by talking, drawing, dancing, singing or playing.

Open Doors for Everyone

Disability inclusion

Every door, every game and every classroom — open to every child!

Fixing play spaces & schools

Adding smooth ramps, handrails and accessible toilets to our buildings, so children using wheelchairs can join in easily.

Talking with community leaders

Chiefs and church and mosque leaders teach everyone that every child is special, equal and loved!

Playing together

Children with special needs join right in with all the fun games and play sessions!

Big words

Disability inclusion — Making sure children with disabilities can join in everything — learning, playing and having fun.

Ramps — A gentle slope instead of steps, so a wheelchair, a pram or wobbly legs can roll or walk right in.

Safe Shield & Helper Teams

Child protection

Grown-ups who keep watch — and help that comes right away!

Community safety talks

Teaching children and adults at schools and neighbourhood centres how to stay safe and where to get help.

Teacher & volunteer training

So teachers and helpers can quickly spot when a child needs help — and protect them.

Caring counselling

Gentle counselling to help children and families heal after tough times.

Quick helper emergency team

An Emergency Fund, so children get medical, legal or safe‑haven support right away!

Help right away
Big words

Counselling — Talking with a kind, trained helper about your feelings, so hard things feel lighter.

Emergency Fund — Money saved up for when a child suddenly needs help, so nobody has to wait.

Why three super powers and not one

You can't build a better Effia-Kwesimintsim with one block

Imagine building Effia-Kwesimintsim out of giant toy blocks. Every colour is one of the three super powers — take a colour away and the whole town wobbles!

1. Happy Playtime2. Open Doors3. Safe Shield

Play dates are great — but not if some children can't get through the door. Ramps help — but not if a child isn't safe at home. When all three super powers work together, every child in Effia-Kwesimintsim grows up happy, safe and ready to learn.
